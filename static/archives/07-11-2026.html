Libghostty 1.0

658 points • by mfiguiere • 36 comments
Summary

Libghostty‑vt 1.0 is a portable, high‑performance C library for terminal emulation extracted from Ghostty. The 1.0 release stabilizes both the library and its C ABI, guaranteeing semantic‑versioning compatibility and allowing dynamic linking without crashes; unsupported calls return GHOSTTY_UNSUPPORTED. It is MIT‑licensed, depends only on libc at runtime, and incorporates permissively‑licensed SIMD (simdutf, Google Highway) and image‑decoding (Wuffs) components, with no copyleft code. The library is already embedded in major products: JetBrains IDE terminals (≈12.5 M users), Neovim (replacing libvterm), KDE Konsole experiments, Vercel Turborepo, dev‑env shells, and numerous commercial tools; community bindings exist for over a dozen languages. Packaging provides signed source and dependency tarballs, shared/static libraries, headers, pkg‑config and CMake files, and supports standard prefix/DESTDIR conventions across Linux, macOS/iOS (XCFramework), Windows (DLL + import lib), Android NDK, and WebAssembly (size‑optimized and speed‑optimized builds). CI tests cover x86_64, aarch64, arm, wasm32 and others; the library is thread‑free, allocator‑agnostic, and demonstrates low memory use (10 k terminals on 4‑core/8 GB server). Documentation and downloads are at lib.ghostty.org.

The Components of Agent Memory

47 points • by geoffbp • 10 comments
Summary

Agent memory is modeled on human hippocampal replay: logs are captured during interaction and later reorganized during idle “dreaming” compute. Memory notes (tagged salient events) sit above raw transcripts, while higher layers include structured representations such as wikis and an injected context layer. Experiments show that letting Claude generate its own salience judgments outperforms fixed rules; models that close the learning loop (e.g., Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5) achieve higher CL‑Bench scores when they write, test, and confirm rules. In Pokémon, a compact, strategy‑focused memory (Fable 5) yields more badges than an exhaustive log (Sonnet 3.7). Dreaming corrects erroneous notes, promotes useful entries, and builds wikis that improve retrieval‑augmented question answering (48 % to 61 % graded criteria on a 350 k‑file corpus). Over 128 dreaming sessions, wiki size grew from 2 M to 128 M tokens, increasing performance while saving ~20 k tokens per query. Proper hierarchy management keeps injected context small, avoiding token bloat and hallucinations.

A Jev that can ask Opus for help gives Opus accuracy at 4x lower cost

484 points • by theanomaly • 41 comments
Summary

The study evaluated LLM decision models (Jev and GPT‑6 Luna) for grading identity‑resolution (IDR) outputs, comparing them to frontier models (Opus). On a benchmark of 1,000 synthetic identities (half correct, half over‑merged) Jev and Luna achieved 88.8‑97.3 % accuracy out‑of‑the‑box, while decision models were up to 90× cheaper ($0.05‑$0.10 per 1 k identities) and 10× faster (~0.15 s). Luna initially outperformed Jev, but its confidence was poorly calibrated, leading to high‑confidence errors on difficult cases. Jev’s confidence remained near 50 % for mistakes, enabling a hybrid pipeline: Jev first, then Opus for low‑confidence cases. This hybrid reached frontier accuracy at > 4× lower cost than Opus alone and maintained scalability. Thresholds of 60 % (Jev) and 70 % (Luna) optimized accuracy. Results suggest calibration is critical; a well‑calibrated cheap model can defer expensive inference selectively, delivering near‑state‑of‑the‑art performance with substantially reduced expense.

128 points • by raybb • 10 comments
Summary

A federal antitrust case alleging that RealPage’s revenue‑management software enabled large apartment owners to coordinate rent increases was settled in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee. The court approved settlements totaling nearly $360 million, with 11 landlords reaching 14 agreements for $218 million in May and 27 landlords entering 26 agreements for $141.8 million in October. The settlement class comprises U.S. multifamily renters who paid rent on properties using RealPage’s Revenue Management Solutions, Lease Rent Options, YieldStar, or AI Revenue Management between Oct. 18 2018 and Nov. 21 2025. Defendants did not admit liability but agreed to cease providing non‑public data to RealPage and to stop using competitor data in pricing recommendations. State attorneys general from D.C., Kentucky, Maryland, New Jersey and Washington challenged the settlements, but Judge Waverly Crenshaw denied their request to reject them. All claims against settling defendants are dismissed with prejudice, while other potential defendants remain subject to separate actions.

Greece has its own Pompeii: Thera buried Akrotiri in ash 17th-16th century BCE

108 points • by derbOac • 8 comments
Summary

The Thera eruption (c. 1620–1530 BCE) blanketed the Bronze‑Age settlement of Akrotiri on present‑day Santorini in ~200 ft of ash and pumice, preserving its structures like a “Pompeii of the Aegean.” The eruption altered the island’s climate, generated tsunamis, and caused the caldera’s collapse, with ash reaching Egypt and Israel. Archaeological work began after a 1866 quarry find; major excavations started in 1967 under Spyridon Marinatos and continue under Christos Doumas, having uncovered roughly 10 % of the site. Findings include multistory stone buildings, sophisticated drainage, and over 30 frescoed rooms—most depicting female‑centered ritual scenes and crocus symbolism—plus pottery, beads, and charred barley and fava beans that illuminate Middle Bronze‑Age diet and trade. No human victims have been found, implying successful evacuation. A bioclimatic shelter protects the ruins, which are open to visitors, while a nearby museum displays artifacts. Akrotiri provides a uniquely detailed view of a cosmopolitan Cycladic society and its connections across the Eastern Mediterranean.
