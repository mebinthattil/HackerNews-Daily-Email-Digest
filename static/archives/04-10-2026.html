OpenBSD Developers Reject Uutils Coreutils

12 points • by pjmlp • 2 comments
Summary

The OpenBSD ports mailing list rejected a proposal to add the Rust‑based uutils coreutils reimplementation. Developer David Uhden Collado submitted a port that installs GNU‑compatible utilities as “g‑” prefixed symlinks to a single multicall binary, conflicting with existing GNU coreutils packages. Senior developers, including founder Theo de Raadt, argued that the port offers unnecessary duplication, introduces subtle behavioral differences, and threatens pipeline reliability because OpenBSD’s base utilities are tightly integrated and tested together. Licensing was dismissed as irrelevant; the existing BSD‑licensed tools already satisfy permissive‑license goals, and adding MIT‑licensed alternatives would create confusion. The Rust implementation raised additional concerns about rapid language releases, large dependency chains, and bootstrapping difficulties on OpenBSD architectures. The multicall design also clashes with OpenBSD’s packaging conventions, which favor individual binaries and incremental updates. Given these technical, compatibility, and maintenance objections, acceptance of the uutils port is considered unlikely without major redesign.
