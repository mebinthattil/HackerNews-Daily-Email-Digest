Summary

Carnival Corporation, the world’s largest cruise operator, emitted roughly ten times more sulfur oxides (SOx) around European coasts in 2017 than all 260 million European cars combined, according to Transport & Environment. Royal Caribbean, the second‑largest operator, emitted about four times the European car fleet’s SOx. SOx forms sulfate aerosols that harm human health and cause acidification. Spain, Italy, Greece, France and Norway experience the highest exposure, with Barcelona, Palma de Mallorca, Venice, Civitavecchia and Southampton identified as the most affected ports. The report notes that lax marine sulfur fuel standards permit cruise ships to burn high‑sulfur bunker fuel along these coastlines. Nitrogen oxides (NOx) from cruise vessels account for ~15 % of Europe’s passenger‑car NOx emissions; in Marseille, 57 ships in 2017 produced nearly a quarter of the NOx emitted by 340 000 cars. Recommendations include adopting a zero‑emission port standard, expanding emission control areas beyond the North and Baltic Seas, and regulating NOx on existing ships. Available technologies—shore‑side electricity, batteries, and hydrogen—are cited as viable solutions, but industry adoption is deemed unlikely without governmental mandates.

Frostbite: A new CPU architecture discovered, designed and verified by AI

134 points • by shioyama • 33 comments
Summary

Frostbite is a general‑purpose CPU built from an interaction‑net programming model, where each computation step is local, dependencies are explicit, and values have single ownership. AI agents autonomously designed the ISA, microarchitecture, RTL, verification flow, and GDS, iterating over compiler, architecture, and runtime specifications encoded in a formal language (ASL) that maps to Lean for theorem proving. The chip, fabricated in 7 nm, runs at 500 MHz; a 2 × 2 tile quad occupies 0.792 mm² and includes on‑chip work‑stealing and a lightweight scheduler, eliminating large caches and coherence logic. Across 25 benchmarks (including FFT, GEMM, LZ4, and recursive algorithms), Frostbite achieved a geometric‑mean 4.59× higher performance‑per‑watt than a modern dual‑issue, in‑order superscalar core of comparable area, with 8.77× higher performance‑per‑area. Energy use shifted from memory fetch/decode (57 % → 38 %) to on‑chip network and scheduler (6 % → 26 %). The AI‑driven workflow proved capable of discovering novel architectural primitives and cross‑stack optimizations unattainable in traditional ASIC flows.

OpenSSL: Buffer overflow in EVP_EncodeBlock() (CVE-2026-121481, CVSS 9.8)

280 points • by JacobHenner • 23 comments
Summary

The pull request backports safeguards for a buffer‑overflow vulnerability (CVE‑2026‑121481) to OpenSSL 3.6.5. Modifications include:

  • EVP_EncodeBlock() now aborts when the input length is negative or when the resulting Base64 output would exceed INT_MAX (dlen > (INT_MAX/4)*3). For empty input the function writes only a NUL terminator.
  • EVP_DecodeBlock() returns 0 for empty input and ‑1 for a negative length, preventing underflow.
  • http_client.c base64encode() validates that the encoded size fits in an int, rejecting inputs that would otherwise overflow (previously only len > INT_MAX was checked). This issue could be triggered via a long s_client -proxy_pass command.

The changes mirror pending upstream guards (openssl/openssl#33652) and are co‑authored by Claude Opus 5.5. No functional code changes beyond the added length checks.

Summary

Jean‑Michel Jarre’s 1976 debut album Oxygène reshaped electronic music. After Polydor’s Fred Haayen secured UK distribution and BBC Radio 1 full‑album play, Oxygène entered the UK chart at #19, spent 24 weeks there and reached the top 10 in seven countries, eventually selling ~18‑20 million copies worldwide. Album art—Michel Granger’s “Oxygène” painting—depicts a skull within Earth and was adapted for the cover. The record’s minimalist, hand‑sequenced structures and novel stereo presentation contributed to its lasting influence on synth‑driven pop.

Show HN: Tinnitus frequency finder and audio notch tool

18 points • by brianrhea • 7 comments
Summary

A browser‑based tool guides users through a four‑minute pitch‑matching procedure to estimate the frequency of their tinnitus. After a safety checklist, users describe the sound (tone, hiss, etc.) and set a quiet reference level. A level check presents nine tones from 1 kHz to 14 kHz, recording the lowest audible point for each; subsequent test sounds are adjusted to equal perceived loudness, compensating for high‑frequency hearing loss. Users then rate up to ten randomly ordered tones (750 Hz–12 kHz) as “far,” “warm,” or “very close,” after which six forced‑choice pairs narrow the estimate from half an octave to about a twelfth. An octave‑verification step compares the provisional match with tones an octave above and below to avoid common octave errors. A second independent match is made by dragging a pure tone to the perceived pitch. The final result shows the median frequency in hertz, a confidence range, and the nearest musical note; results are stored locally and averaged across sessions. The service is free, requires headphones, and does not replace medical evaluation.

µ-Wire – USB on an ATtiny 10

50 points • by mofosyne • 4 comments
Summary

The ATtiny10, a 6‑pin SOT23 AVR with 1 KB flash, 32 B SRAM and a reduced 16‑register core, was used to implement a USB‑compatible “µ‑Wire” device that drives a single WS2812 RGB LED. By stripping V‑USB to its essentials—removing interrupt handling, reset processing, double buffering, and non‑essential endpoints—the firmware fits within the ATtiny10’s limits (988 / 1024 B flash, 28 B variables + 2 B stack SRAM). An interrupt‑free V‑USB variant from micronucleus reduced SRAM use, while manual register remapping avoided register collisions. The author calibrated the internal 12 MHz RC oscillator for USB timing, accepting the lack of a PLL. AVR‑GCC’s failure to emit the one‑word LDS/STS instructions for the reduced core forced inline assembly macros to achieve optimal code size. The final implementation processes USB SETUP packets in‑order, stealing time to update the WS2812 while ignoring the following data packet, enabling full protocol compatibility with existing little‑wire host software. Source code is available on GitHub.
