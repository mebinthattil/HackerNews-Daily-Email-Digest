  • gap‑decorations (rule, row‑rule, column‑rule) to render grid borders directly.
  • scroll‑state container queries for detecting when position:sticky elements are stuck or when a scroll container reaches top/bottom.

Most proposals are usable today as progressive enhancements in browsers that already support them, and many are expected to enter Interop 2027 for broader adoption.

In Hilbert space, all things are quantumly possible

87 points • by unprovable • 9 comments
Summary

Hilbert space is defined by two mathematical criteria: completeness (no missing points) and the existence of an inner product that quantifies alignment between states and axes. Its dimension reflects the number of possible outcomes for a system—e.g., a qubit has a two‑dimensional space, a particle with unrestricted position has an infinite‑dimensional space. The same Hilbert space underlies different measurement bases, allowing Heisenberg’s picture (rotating axes) and Schrödinger’s picture (rotating state vectors) to describe identical physics. Von Neumann named these abstract spaces after Hilbert, who first studied infinite‑dimensional examples.

Philosophically, some, like Sean Carroll, treat Hilbert space as the fundamental arena of reality, while others, such as Jonathan Sorce, view it as a useful but not universal mathematical tool, preferring alternative structures when needed. One such structure is the von Neumann algebra, which describes transformations of Hilbert spaces and has been explored to resolve logical issues and to model quantum aspects of spacetime and black holes. Researchers thus employ a “zoo” of spaces—Hilbert spaces, von Neumann algebras, and others—depending on the quantum system under study.

The article introduces C++‑26 std::polymorphic as a value‑type alternative to std::unique_ptr + virtual clone() for owning heterogeneous components. It supports small‑buffer optimisation, allocator awareness, and provides operator*, operator->, and valueless_after_move(). The article advises using std::polymorphic for value‑semantic owners with runtime‑varying types, std::indirect for fixed types, std::variant for closed sets, and smart pointers for shared identity.
