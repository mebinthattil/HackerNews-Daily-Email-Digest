An anti-slavery sugar bowl (2024)

It reflects the anti‑slavery movement’s strategy of embedding political messages in everyday domestic items to influence consumer behaviour. Most abolitionist sugar bowls were ceramic; this glass example illustrates how manufacturers benefited financially from both the slave‑produced sugar trade and the subsequent boycotts. How a Mechanical Watch Keeps Time (Interactive 3D)

34 points • by martincmartin • 4 comments
Summary

Mechanical watches use a calibrated oscillating system to regulate time. A hairspring— a fine steel spiral—provides a restoring torque, while the attached balance wheel supplies inertia, together creating a regular beat (typically one swing every two seconds). The escapement converts this oscillation into incremental steps: the balance’s impulse jewel strikes a pallet fork, allowing the escape wheel to advance one tooth per swing, producing the audible “tick‑tock.” A gear train reduces the escape wheel’s speed: a pinion drives a larger fourth wheel that turns once per minute, moving the second hand. Subsequent gear reductions (7½ × 8) yield the minute wheel, turning once per hour, and a further 3 × 4 reduction provides the hour wheel, completing a 12:1 ratio for a 12‑hour rotation. Power originates from a mainspring, a coiled steel spring wound by a key; its slow unwind drives a barrel that engages the gear train. Properly adjusted, this fully mechanical system can achieve ~99.99 % accuracy, losing or gaining about nine seconds per day.
