Universal XSS in uBlock Origin from website-driven element picker

410 points • by zaik • 29 comments
Summary

The post details a universal XSS vulnerability in the Firefox MV2 version of uBlock Origin (and a lesser‑impact variant in MV3/uBO Lite). The extension’s element picker, invoked via right‑click, is implemented by loading an iframe from a URL of the form moz-extension://<UUID>/web_accessible_resources/epicker‑ui.html. An attacker can discover the extension’s random UUID through a built‑in “no‑fetch‑if” filter that redirects ad‑script requests to a local script, intercepting the resulting XHR. With the UUID, the attacker embeds the picker iframe invisibly (opacity 0) and communicates via postMessage and a MessageChannel. By supplying a crafted opt field containing a newline, the attacker injects an arbitrary trusted filter such as google.com##+js(trusted‑rpnt, script, …), which the picker accepts and adds as a trusted filter. When the user clicks the hidden “Create” button, the filter is stored, allowing the attacker to execute JavaScript on any site. The flaw was patched by requiring a secret token from the content script in the picker’s initial message.

Researchers track whole body movement with just a smartwatch

70 points • by PaulHoule • 8 comments
Summary

Researchers at Cornell’s SciFi Lab introduced EchoMotion, a system that uses ultrasonic sound emitted and captured by a single commercial smartwatch (speaker and microphone) to infer full‑body pose. A deep‑learning model translates echo patterns into the positions of 12 joints, achieving average errors under four inches (≈10 cm) for new users, reduced to ~2.4 inches after a 12‑minute calibration. The prototype consumed ~12 mW, using about 5‑6 % of a Galaxy Watch 6 battery for a 30‑minute session. Tests on unmodified Galaxy Watch 6, Watch 7, and Google Pixel Watch 3 confirmed consistent performance across devices. Experiments with 10 volunteers performing 18 exercises showed robustness to typical indoor environments and ambient noise, with slight degradation in hallways due to external echoes. The researchers aim to apply EchoMotion to fitness tracking, rehabilitation, and integration with smart‑glasses vision, leveraging its privacy‑preserving, low‑data, low‑power characteristics. The work was presented at ACM UbiComp/IWSC and funded by the NSF.

Exact Gradients Through a BRep Kernel

13 points • by ecto • 0 comments
Summary

The paper presents a method for computing exact gradients of design‑parameter‑dependent objectives in a boundary‑representation (BRep) kernel by freezing the tessellation topology and differentiating node positions. Surface nodes are differentiated using dual numbers on analytic surfaces, while vertex and trim nodes are obtained via implicit differentiation of surface equations, with added tangency constraints for fillet blends. The approach yields exact first‑order derivatives for the frozen mesh, supports directional second‑order derivatives, and enables a transposed (reverse‑mode) pass that computes per‑parameter cotangents in a single forward evaluation. It integrates mass‑property and rigid‑body dynamics sensitivities, including a differentiable penalty‑contact model, and reports topology changes as errors. Validation against central finite differences shows errors dominated by polygonization (e.g., 6.7 % at 16 segments per circle, 0.11 % at 128). Limitations include dependence on fixed topology, small valid parameter steps (≈ mm), and the need to recompute a fresh plan each iteration.

Old Bitly links are being hijacked

305 points • by hspeiser • 29 comments
Summary

Bitly disclosed a vulnerability that let attackers re‑register the back‑half of old, inactive bit.ly links and redirect them to arbitrary content. On 15 Nov 2026 the short links in a 2011 Burger King tweet (now pointing to Wikipedia’s list of Nazi camps) and a 2011 McDonald’s tweet (now pointing to Wikipedia’s September 11 article) were claimed and later deactivated by Bitly. An analysis of 97 bit.ly links from Donald Trump’s 2009‑2013 tweets found 40 still original, 49 re‑created later but still directing to the intended pages (mostly YouTube or news sites), and 7 re‑created to unrelated destinations (now disabled). CNN reported similar hijacks affecting posts from Bill Gates, Justin Trudeau, Mike Pence, the FBI and the U.S. Department of Defense. Bitly’s help pages claim back‑halves cannot be reused; a bug that allowed this was reportedly fixed in Feb 2025, yet the issue re‑emerged. Recommendations include auditing historic short links, verifying creation dates via a “+” suffix, removing compromised links, and using owned short domains for future URLs.
