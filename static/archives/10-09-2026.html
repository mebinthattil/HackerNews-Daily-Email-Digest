The Larger Pacific striped octopus (LPSO), also called the Harlequin octopus, is an undescribed cephalopod lacking an official scientific name. Documented since the 1970s, it inhabits tropical Eastern Pacific waters—from the Bay of Panama to northern Colombia—preferring soft mud or mud‑sand substrates at 7–100 m depth. Unlike most octopuses, LPSO forms social colonies of up to 40 individuals, with dens spaced within ~1 m and lifelong cohabitation. It displays three body‑color patterns (pale, dark brown, and a distinctive stripe‑bar‑spot pattern) that serve as individual identifiers. Mating occurs via a beak‑to‑beak embrace; females are non‑cannibalistic, and the species can spawn multiple times, laying paired eggs that females tend until hatching. Hatchlings are pelagic. LPSO preys on shrimp, stomatopods, crabs, and bivalves, employing stalking, chasing, ambush, and drilling tactics. The lifespan is about two years; senescence lasts longer in females (2–4 months) than males (1–2 weeks).

What do Visa and Mastercard do? An intro to card networks

503 points • by evakhoury • 31 comments
Summary

Visa and Mastercard function as card networks, not issuers, banks, processors, or hardware manufacturers. Their core responsibilities are:

  • Telecommunications: Operate secure data‑center networks that route transaction messages (authorizations) between merchants’ acquirers and card issuers, using PAN/BIN identifiers.
  • Financial coordination: Facilitate settlement by netting daily debits and credits, moving funds domestically and internationally, and maintaining liquidity (Visa held $11.2 B in 2024 with peak daily exposure of $137.4 B).
  • Incentive structuring: Set interchange fees and network assessment fees that influence issuer, merchant, and consumer behavior; typical U.S. split: merchant pays ~2.5 % (MDR), of which ~2 % goes to the issuer, ~0.15 % to the network.
  • Rule enforcement and dispute handling: Publish comprehensive network rules, impose compliance penalties, and provide a forced‑arbitration dispute process that can involve fees for review.

These functions create a two‑sided market linking cardholders, issuers, merchants, and acquirers, enabling widespread card acceptance and supporting the global payment ecosystem.
