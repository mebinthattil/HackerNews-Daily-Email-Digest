Wanted to download an ISO, ended up writing a kernel exploit

36 points • by 0xdeafbeef • 7 comments
Summary

The author bought a TP‑Link EAP770 v1 (Qualcomm IPQ9574, Linux 5.4.164) that suffered silent data corruption on large TCP/HTTPS transfers over Wi‑Fi. Debugging showed 64‑byte blocks of zero‑filled data within otherwise checksum‑valid TCP segments, causing TLS/SSH “bad record mac” errors. The corruption appeared only on bridged LAN‑to‑Wi‑Fi traffic, implicating the AP’s Qualcomm SFE/PPE offload path.

Investigation revealed a DMA cache‑coherency bug in the out‑of‑tree qca‑nss‑dp driver: packet buffers are recycled without full cache invalidation, so stale cache lines can be written back after NIC DMA, overwriting received payloads. The offload then recomputes TCP checksums, delivering valid but corrupted data to clients.

Fixing required disabling two sysfs controls (/sys/sfe/l2_feature and /sys/sfe/ppe_rfs_feature), which are root‑only and inaccessible via TP‑Link’s restricted shell. The author crafted a kernel privilege‑escalation exploit using CVE‑2022‑0185 (integer underflow in legacy_parse_param) and pipe‑buffer manipulation to perform an arbitrary write, set those sysfs values to 0, and reboot‑persist the change with a watchdog script. Disabling the offload eliminates the corruption at a modest (~8 %) performance cost; uploads and wired traffic were unaffected. The bug is specific to Qualcomm’s SFE/PPE implementation and not present in x86 or OpenWrt builds.

It extends the LFM2.5 line, featuring a 40‑layer architecture, 64 experts per MoE block with top‑4 routing, gated short convolutions, and GQA. Key upgrades include a context window enlarged from 32 k to 131 k tokens and a vocabulary doubled to 128 k tokens for improved non‑Latin tokenization. Pre‑training scaled from 17 T to 33 T tokens, followed by a two‑phase mid‑training (2 T tokens at 32 k context, then 300 B tokens at 131 k) emphasizing reasoning, math, code, and long‑context data, plus tool‑call trajectories. A five‑stage fine‑tuning pipeline (SFT, teacher specialization, non‑agentic and agentic MOPD, agentic RL) produces the final reasoning‑only model that outputs explicit chains of thought.

Benchmarking on 16 tasks shows competitive results versus larger MoEs (e.g., Qwen3.6‑35B‑A3B), leading on five of eight tool‑use/agentic benchmarks (AutomationBench, BFCLv4, ToolSandbox, LiveClaw, τ³‑Bench Banking) and all instruction‑following tests. Inference speeds reach 184 tokens /s on Apple M5 Max, 114 tokens /s on AMD Ryzen AI Max+, and >21 k output tokens /s on a single NVIDIA B300 GPU, up to 1.7× faster than peers. The model, base and fine‑tuned weights, are open‑weight on Hugging Face with support for llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM, SGLang, and ONNX. Pricing is $0.10 per M input tokens and $1.00 per M output tokens.
