Pill to treat pancreatic cancer hailed as 'biggest breakthrough in decades'

1056 points • by rafaelc • 25 comments
Summary

A phase‑II trial of daraxonrasib, a KRAS inhibitor that blocks oncogenic KRAS mutations common in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, reported notable tumor responses in patients with newly diagnosed, metastatic disease. Among 61 participants, tumor growth halted in ~90 % of cases; >60 % experienced tumor shrinkage or disappearance, and ~8 % achieved radiographic complete response (tumors undetectable on CT). After a median 18‑month follow‑up, 76.6 % of patients were alive at 12 months, compared with the typical 40‑45 % survival for chemotherapy alone. One in 12 patients achieved undetectable disease. Daraxonrasib’s mechanism—binding and inactivating mutant KRAS—suggests potential applicability to other KRAS‑driven cancers. Researchers emphasized that the drug is not yet a cure, long‑term durability remains uncertain, and larger studies are required before regulatory approval and NHS adoption. Pancreatic cancer currently has a 5‑year survival of ~3 % when metastatic, highlighting the significance of these early efficacy signals.

A real Linux kernel, compiled to WebAssembly

41 points • by DonAtimator • 4 comments
Summary

Unkernel compiles a full Linux 7.x kernel (including syscalls, scheduler, ext4, TCP/IP, MMU with Sv39 page tables) into a single WebAssembly module that runs entirely in the browser or Node. User programs are unmodified aarch64 binaries (Alpine, Debian, etc.) executed by kwemu, an AArch64 CPU written in Zig and linked into the kernel; memory management and syscalls are handled by the native Linux code, not emulated. Boot completes in ~70 ms, the Alpine shell appears in 0.3 s after a ~10 MB download, and the kernel runs with 48 MiB guest memory. Measured syscall latency is ~170 ns (vs 110 ns native), read 64 B ≈170 ns, and fork‑exit‑wait ≈60 µs, comparable to native and far faster than QEMU. JIT‑compiled hot user code yields 1.6–2.3× speed‑up over interpretation; OpenSSL achieves ~100 MiB/s AES‑128‑GCM and ~160 MiB/s SHA‑256. Features include ext4/overlayfs/tmpfs, full TCP/IP stack, cgroups, namespaces, vsock, ALSA sound, framebuffer, and persistent storage via IndexedDB or host‑provided images. Limitations: no IPv6, limited UDP/DHCP/DNS, no inbound connections, missing ptrace, seccomp, and some SIMD fallback. The MIT‑licensed JavaScript SDK provides CLI, TypeScript API, and worker‑based SMP support, enabling embedding Linux shells, package managers, graphics, sound, and custom back‑ends in web or server environments.
