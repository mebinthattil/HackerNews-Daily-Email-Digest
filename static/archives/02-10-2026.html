How Singapore's government-run dating service works

164 points • by danielfoster • 27 comments
Summary

Singapore’s GovTech has launched “FirstDate,” a pilot matchmaking service for government employees that uses Singpass for identity verification and the Gale‑Shapley Stable Marriage Algorithm to generate one stable match per cycle. Participants complete a detailed questionnaire covering interests, cuisine preferences, travel habits, lifestyle, love languages, and personal values; the data are used to rank opposite‑gender users and produce stable pairings. The article explains the algorithm’s origins in medical‑resident placement and school‑choice systems, noting it guarantees no mutually preferable pairings but does not ensure satisfaction, especially when participants have external options or when preferences are ambiguous. Critics highlight three issues: (1) stability does not equal happiness; (2) questionnaire responses may not reflect true preferences; (3) reliance on checklist‑style matching mirrors commercial dating apps and may reinforce transactional views of relationships. The piece argues that Singapore’s broader dating challenges stem from declining “third spaces” and suggests investing in cultural, social, and recreational venues to foster organic connections rather than relying on algorithmic solutions.

Summary

Cloudflare released two open‑source decision models, Clef and Clef‑flash, on Workers AI and Hugging Face (Apache 2.0). Decision models output bounded, typed classifications with probability scores, enabling programmatic routing without continual retraining. Clef incorporates a vision encoder and a 64 k token context window (vs 32 k for Jev), and is built on frozen Qwen 3.8‑27B (Clef) and Qwen 3.5‑9B (Clef‑flash) backbones with low‑rank adapters, a two‑stage attention‑routing head, label‑smoothed cross‑entropy, Brier loss, and RL‑based calibration (RLCD). Benchmarks on the Jev Decision Index show Clef achieving top scores (e.g., 98.47 % case‑exact on BFCL, 91.93 % API‑Bank accuracy) and median latency of 209 ms, while Clef‑flash reaches 38.8 ms median latency with comparable quality. Hosted at the edge, the models halve latency versus a 120 B LLM in a Threat‑Intelligence domain‑classification workflow (2.2 s vs 4.7 s). Cloudflare also offers a reinforcement‑learning fine‑tuning service—initially via a forward‑deployed‑engineer team, later self‑serve—leveraging AI Gateway, Workers AI, Containers, and a new Trainer to capture data, fine‑tune, and redeploy customized Clef models.

Pi Durable

380 points • by paulsmith • 16 comments
Summary

Pi Durable is a framework for running Pi coding agents as long‑running, durable, and malleable applications. It provides a “harness” that stores conversations and tasks, and supplies execution environments for tools. Storage backends include in‑memory, SQLite, and JSONL; the interface is minimal and can be implemented on key‑value stores or Postgres, allowing deployment on any JavaScript runtime (Node, Bun, Cloudflare Durable Objects). Only active transcripts, live tasks, and pending submissions are kept in memory; older messages are compacted to stay within model context limits. Each step is a checkpointed task, so crashes are recovered by reopening the same storage and resuming unfinished tasks, with exact‑once request IDs preventing duplicate submissions. Multiple conversations run concurrently, can fork from any transcript point, and maintain independent agents (model, tools, settings). Extensions bundle system‑prompt sections, tools, hooks, and tasks; they are pluggable, can wrap or replace tools, and participate in durability. Tools are durable tasks with replay safety flags, and can spawn sub‑agents (sub‑conversations) that survive crashes. Hooks allow custom logic on model generation, tool invocation, or compaction, storing decisions memoized per task. The entire codebase is ~15 k lines (~150 k‑250 k tokens).
