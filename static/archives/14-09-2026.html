Summary

The post documents how Claude Fable 5.1 solved Sir Thomas Urquhart’s “Cyphral Distich,” a 64‑number cryptogram that had been listed among unsolved historical ciphers. The key clue is the reference to Urquhart’s 32 “Proquiritations”; each number in the two lines indexes a word in the corresponding Proquiritation, and the first letter of that word forms the plaintext. Decoding yields the 32‑letter verse “O GOD UPHOLD KING CHARLS THE SECOND AND MAKE HIM THE SUPREME RULER OF THIS LAND,” a royalist prayer consistent with Urquhart’s politics.

Using the same method, Fable 5.1 also solved the larger “Cyphral Octastich” (285 numbers) from Urquhart’s The Jewel (1652). Here the k‑th number indexes a word on page k of the book; extracting first letters produces an ottava rima royalist prayer, with only nine letters remaining ambiguous due to possible transcription errors.

The author argues this demonstrates that large language models can resolve archival puzzles by persistent, targeted search, not by novel cryptanalytic techniques.

The case against JPEG XL

151 points • by contact9879 • 28 comments
Summary

JPEG XL is a royalty‑free image codec that offers lossless compression roughly 12 % smaller than lossless WebP, but its lossless advantage applies to a very small portion of Web content. In lossy mode it lags behind AVIF/AV1: it lacks directional prediction, deblocking loop filtering, and efficient edge‑preserving tools, resulting in higher bitrates and poorer handling of non‑photographic images. Its “XYB” color space provides limited gains, and encoder‑side features such as splines and patches add complexity without matching AV1’s intra‑block copy efficiency. Decode speed is slower than AVIF and significantly slower than WebP; progressive decoding is poorly supported, and crafted images can cause extreme decode times (e.g., a 1.9 KB image taking >17 s), posing a denial‑of‑service risk. While JPEG XL’s flexible spec (up to 4096 channels, HDR, JPEG recompression) is technically impressive, the Web’s primary needs are bandwidth reduction, fast decode, and minimal compatibility overhead—requirements that AVIF already satisfies. Consequently, JPEG XL may be valuable for professional or non‑Web use cases, but it offers no compelling advantage for current browser adoption.
