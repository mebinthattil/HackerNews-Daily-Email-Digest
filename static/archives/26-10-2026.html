The result of the C++26 DIS ballot

40 points • by uasi • 10 comments
Summary

C++26’s DIS ballot failed on 23 Oct 2026, marking the first ISO/IEC SC22 draft to miss both required thresholds (14/20 favorable votes and ≤5 negative out of 22). The draft received 12 “yes” votes and nine “no” votes from nine countries (Denmark, Finland, Iran, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Romania, Spain, Sweden); France and Ireland abstained with comments, Poland and the US abstained without comment. The primary source of opposition appears to be the contracts feature, which critics argue lacks sufficient implementation guarantees and raises semantic choices (ignore, observe, enforce, quick‑enforce). The ISO directives allow three remedial paths: issue a revised enquiry draft (8‑week vote), circulate a revised committee draft, or publish as a technical specification; cancellation requires SC22 approval. WG21 will discuss options at the Búzios meeting (16‑21 Nov 2026). Meanwhile, compilers continue to ship draft features (GCC 16 with ‑std=c++26, Clang with ‑std=c++2c), but these implementations are not guaranteed to match any future standard.

Growing Trees

45 points • by ibobev • 4 comments
Summary

The implementation follows Palubicki et al.’s self‑organizing tree model. Growth proceeds in discrete seasons on a metameric structure (internode + leaf + bud). Each season: (1) light is sampled per bud using a voxel‑grid shadow model where each bud casts a decaying pyramid; buds also read a direction vector V toward the least‑shaded voxel. (2) Light values are summed from tips to root, giving a base resource v = α Q_base. (3) Resource ascends the hierarchy, split at each branch according to the extended Borchert‑Honda equation v_m = v · λ Q_m / (λ Q_m + (1‑λ)Q_l); λ controls bias toward the main axis. Buds receiving ≥1 unit generate ⌊v⌋ new metamers (subject to a cap). (4) New segment direction is a weighted sum of previous direction, V, tropism, and noise; phyllotactic bud placement (spiral, opposite, whorl) is parallel‑transported along the shoot. (5) Branches are pruned when average light per metamer falls below a threshold; shed branches still contribute to trunk girth. Girth is computed with an apipe model (r_parentⁿ = ∑ r_iⁿ, n = 2 for area preservation). The resulting skeleton is rendered as a swept bark mesh; foliage uses twig‑spray cards drawn from a procedural atlas, with normal blending toward a crown ellipsoid and distance‑based LOD (cards → impostors). The system can bake depth‑field views for efficient forest instancing.

Overgrown, when plants invade your abandoned tabs

264 points • by speckx • 23 comments
Summary

A Chrome extension named “Overgrown” visualizes neglected browser tabs as being overtaken by plant growth. Each tab runs a neglect timer that increments when the tab is out of view and pauses when active. As the timer progresses, SVG plant graphics expand from the edges toward the center; once over 5 % coverage, the page becomes non‑interactive (scrolling only). Users clear the vines by dragging the cursor across them, reducing coverage back to 5 % before interaction is restored. The extension includes configurable settings for growth speed and selectable “weapons” (rusty machete, chainsaw, flamethrower) that affect the clearing animation. Architecturally, the code is divided into three components: a background script (background.js) that initializes and removes per‑tab state, a popup UI for time, weapon, and tab status controls, and a content script folder containing SVG assets, growth logic, the neglect clock, and sound effects.
