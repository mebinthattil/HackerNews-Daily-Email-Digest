Summary

Margaret Hamilton, MIT Instrumentation Laboratory leader, pioneered defensive and priority‑driven software for NASA’s Apollo Guidance Computer. She introduced error‑prevention measures after a “Lauren error” where a child inadvertently launched a pre‑flight program, later validating the fix when astronaut Jim Lovell repeated the mistake on Apollo 8. Her team’s priority‑driven code enabled the Apollo 11 lunar landing by suppressing non‑essential tasks during a 1202 overload alarm, allowing mission‑critical operations to continue. After Apollo, Hamilton remained at the lab until its 1970s spin‑off into Draper Laboratory, then founded Higher Order Software (1976) and Hamilton Technologies (1990s). Hamilton Technologies’ flagship, the Universal Systems Language (USL), implements her fault‑tolerant, model‑based approach to complex software. She advocated for “software engineering” as a distinct discipline, earning honors including the NASA Exceptional Space Act Award (2003), Computer History Museum Fellow (2017), and induction into the National Aviation Hall of Fame (2022). Her work has become a touchstone for reliability‑focused software development and for women in computing.

Classic PC demoscene productions running natively in the browser

78 points • by adunk • 11 comments
Summary

The project recompiles classic DOS demoscene productions so they execute directly in a web browser. An x86 emulator runs the original demo, recording every executed code block and its exact cycle timing. This trace is converted into C code that mirrors the original instructions one‑for‑one, then compiled to WebAssembly along with software models of the timer, VGA card, and Sound Blaster hardware. The WebAssembly output is validated against the emulator by comparing interrupts, port accesses, and video frames at each emulated moment, ensuring cycle‑accurate behavior. Demos run at their native 70 Hz VGA timing, appearing smoothest on displays of 70 Hz or higher. The site hosts four free releases: Unreal and Second Reality (Future Crew), Crystal Dream II (Triton), and Stars: Wonders of the World (NoooN), using unmodified original files. Visitor counts are anonymous, with no IP or cookie tracking. The displayed font is IBM VGA 8×16 by VileR (CC BY‑SA 4.0).
