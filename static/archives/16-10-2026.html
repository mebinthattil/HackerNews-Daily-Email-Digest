Last rites for Gentoo's Chromium package

43 points • by signa11 • 5 comments
Summary

Chromium, Google’s open‑source browser, has been declared “last rites” in Gentoo, meaning its ebuild is masked and the package is unmaintained. The difficulty stems from Chromium’s complex build system, bundled toolchain, frequent releases, and new Rust and Go dependencies, which require extensive compile‑time configuration via USE flags. Gentoo’s source‑based model allows users to select optional libraries and toolchains, but maintaining a fully functional ebuild across architectures (x86‑64, arm64, ppc64, RISC‑V) demands multiple skilled volunteers familiar with C, C++, Go, JavaScript, Rust, and Google’s proprietary tools. Attempts to adopt newer ebuilds from third‑party overlays (Bentoo, Fireburn) have failed or required substantial testing, and the upstream build process is unreliable, producing incomplete tarballs. Consequently, Gentoo users must obtain Chromium via alternatives such as Flatpak, pre‑built binaries (x86‑64 only), or other distributions (Arch, Debian, Fedora). Restoration would need a dedicated maintainer team and a reduction of optional features, but no viable plan exists currently.

RPKI now protects 70% of internet routes

142 points • by emot • 5 comments
Summary

RPKI now validates 70.3 % of routed IPv4 prefixes (up from 54 % in Sep 2025), a 16.4‑point gain driven largely by China, whose valid IPv4 coverage rose from 3.7 % to 83 % in one year. Invalid routes are seen by only two of the 1.6 M BGP collectors, indicating they rarely propagate. Country‑level invalid‑prefix counts can be misleading; weighting by address count and visibility reduces Spain’s invalid share from 28.2 % to 0.057 %. IPv6 RPKI coverage outpaces IPv4, reaching 79 % valid prefixes. About 16.2 % of RPKI‑covered IPv4 routes have “open windows” (maxLength set), allowing more‑specific hijacks such as the Hetzner incident; hosting providers vary widely in exposure. RPKI coverage can drop when ROAs disappear, as seen in Mauritius, Saudi Telecom, SEACOM, and Orange Maroc. ASPA (provider‑authorization) records grew from 131 to 3 272 networks, raising ASPA‑valid routing from 6.2 % to 9.7 % (≈3.5 % of traffic now covered). Recommendations: publish ROAs for all announcements, leave maxLength unset, monitor ROA continuity, and publish complete ASPA records.
