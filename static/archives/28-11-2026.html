Loom seamlessly combines weaving, knitting and braiding

153 points • by PaulHoule • 10 comments
Summary

Cornell researchers have created “Kneadix,” a prototype loom that merges weaving, braiding and knit‑like processes by treating them as yarn‑twisting operations. The machine replaces a traditional heddle with a bed of rotating hexagonal horn gears from braiding equipment, allowing any yarn to alternate between warp and weft mid‑fabric and enabling curved edges. A retractable comb with adjustable teeth replaces the standard beater, and each yarn carrier includes a sensor‑driven tensioner that winds or unwinds yarn to maintain optimal tension and friction. Programming the loom currently requires manual yarn tracking; the team provides a Python module library and plans to automate tracking and develop a more intuitive interface. Potential applications include high‑performance garments (e.g., gloves combining knit, woven and braided sections) and smart clothing with integrated electrical pathways. Future work aims to improve stability, expand yarn compatibility, and release a 3D‑printable kit for broader use. The project was led by François Guimbretière with support from NSF, Cornell Bowers Undergraduate Research Experience and a design‑tech seed grant.

Google's DeepCrossAttention achieves comparable model quality up to 3x faster

448 points • by ComputedCamp995 • 16 comments
Summary

The paper “DeepCrossAttention: Supercharging Transformer Residual Connections” critiques standard transformer residual (skip) connections, which simply add a layer’s output to its input with equal weighting, causing dilution of early‑layer details in deep networks. To address this, the authors propose DeepCrossAttention (DCA), a generalized residual mechanism that learns dynamic routing weights for features from multiple preceding layers, allowing each layer to select the most relevant historical representations. Experimental results on language‑modeling benchmarks (LM1B, C4) show DCA reduces validation perplexity compared with baseline Transformers and recent alternatives (LAUREL‑PA, 1×1‑DenseFormer, Hyper‑Connections). Performance improves as the number of active routing connections (k) increases (e.g., perplexity drops from 15.14 to 14.35 when k rises from 1 to 24). The approach also achieves comparable or better accuracy while decreasing parameter count by 30‑40 %. Visual figures illustrate the routing architecture, benchmark comparisons, and the effect of varying k on perplexity.
