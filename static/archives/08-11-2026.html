Agent memory needs its OpenTelemetry moment

32 points • by mthines • 18 comments
Summary

The post highlights the fragmentation of agent memory storage across many proprietary formats—AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, .cursor rules, custom “/memories” directories, Copilot’s GitHub‑side facts, and JSONL knowledge graphs—preventing tools from sharing learned data. It argues that, like observability before 2019, the field lacks a common data model, semantic conventions, and protocol. The author cites OpenTelemetry’s successful merger of OpenTracing and OpenCensus as a template: a shared model (spans, metrics, logs), standard names (service.name, http.*), a single transport (OTLP), and reference SDKs, while vendors compete on storage and analysis. Existing memory proposals (Portable Memory, Open Memory Protocol, COGX, LangGraph, etc.) each solve export but not live addressing, and differ on address levels, identity handling, lifecycle, and author attribution. A spec should define address hierarchy, record schema (id, address, key, lifecycle, written_by, content_type, attributes), conventions, MCP‑based operations, and conformance tests, leaving storage, ranking, and UI to competition. The post calls for a neutral working group to develop this shared standard.

Kirlian Photography: Building Your Own Equipment

50 points • by joebig • 3 comments
Summary

Kirlian photography is a high‑voltage, contact‑print technique that records corona discharge on photographic film. Originating with S. Kirlian and Valentina in 1939, it follows earlier electro‑photography work by Lictenberg, Tesla, Narkevich‑Todka, and F.F. Strong. The basic setup places sheet film on a metal (discharge) plate, positions the object on the film, and applies a brief high‑voltage pulse; the discharge pattern is captured on the film without a lens. Inanimate subjects are grounded to enhance the corona, while living subjects must not be grounded for safety. Black‑and‑white ortho film is recommended for beginners because it can be developed under a red safelight; color film requires total darkness.

A simple manual device can be built from two 67.5 V batteries (or series‑wired 9 V cells), a high‑voltage autotransformer, capacitors, and a momentary toggle switch. A transparent discharge plate—glass coated with tin‑oxide, surrounded by a copper ring electrode, and sealed between thin plastic sheets with silicone—allows use of a standard camera (bulb mode) or video camera. Proper insulation, grounding methods, and exposure timing (10–20 s or multiple switch pulses) are essential. Reported “paranormal” claims, such as disease prediction or the “phantom leaf” effect, are explained by variable physical factors (skin resistance, humidity, pressure, voltage, frequency) rather than bio‑plasma phenomena.
