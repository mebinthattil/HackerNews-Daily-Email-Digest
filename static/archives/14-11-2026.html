What happened to 3Com?

149 points • by gvieri • 25 comments
Summary

3Com, founded in 1979 by Ethernet co‑inventor Bob Metcalfe, grew to a $5.8 billion networking firm during the dot‑com boom, known for reliable, U.S.-manufactured network cards and a strong brand alongside Cisco and Intel. After the bust, the company abandoned its enterprise networking segment, losing revenue and dealer support, while competitors like Intel undercut its pricing. Diversification moves—most notably the costly acquisition of US Robotics and entry into the dial‑up modem market just before broadband emergence—further strained profitability; 3Com posted no profit from 2000 to 2005. Attempts at overseas production and a partnership with Huawei failed to reverse the decline. In late 2009, Hewlett‑Packard acquired 3Com’s assets for $2.7 billion, integrating the technology into HP’s Aruba‑branded networking line; HP now sells Intel‑based NICs under its own label. Vintage 3Com cards remain popular among retro‑computing enthusiasts for their driver support and reliability.
